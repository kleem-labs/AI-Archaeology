# The Living Mathematics

Imagine that every formula in this book has vanished.

The machines are gone too. No NumPy. No PyTorch. No names such as *vector*,
*entropy*, or *attention*. What remains is a world that refuses to stop asking
questions: tracks cross a valley, words change meaning inside sentences,
lanterns preserve several possible stories, and actions alter the future that
will later be measured.

That is where AI Archaeology begins.

We will not tour a museum of finished equations. We will walk through a world
in which mathematics is still being invented. An old instrument will work for
a while. A small, stubborn example will break it. You will be able to place a
finger over the missing distinction, watch the answer collapse, and then lift
your finger as the repair appears. Only afterward will symbols give the journey
a shorter name.

These places are not costumes placed over technical material. Each keeps a
different kind of mathematical promise. The valley preserves what was
observed. The Scriptorium preserves relationships among meanings. The
Observatory refuses to extinguish possibilities too early. The Gatehouse asks
which arrows are permitted to become real actions. The Engine Cavern allows a
mechanism to change while its responsibility remains fixed.

When a place returns, it should look different because *you* are different.
After distance, two marks on the dust-map can be separated. After derivatives,
the same separation can be asked how it would change. After optimization, it
can guide a step. The landscape remembers every instrument we earn.

## The motions beneath the symbols

A symbol is easiest to remember when the hand can still feel the motion that
created it.

| Mathematical motion | Image to carry | The exact promise it keeps |
|---|---|---|
| Add or sum | A chorus | Many witnesses may contribute to one answer; one silent witness must not erase the others. |
| Subtract | A chisel | Remove what two cases share so their remaining difference becomes visible. |
| Multiply | A lock and key | One influence acts through another; if either factor is absent, that path contributes nothing. |
| Divide | A fair cup | Judge a total per example, per unit, or per step so size alone cannot impersonate quality. |
| Square | An echoing chamber | Opposite departures stop cancelling, while large departures return with greater force. |
| Take a square root | The road home | Return a squared construction to the scale of the world that produced it. |
| Exponentiate | A rising flame | Turn unrestricted scores into positive relative evidence and magnify meaningful differences. |
| Take a logarithm | A spiral stair | Turn multiplied chances into steps that can be added and make confident neglect visibly costly. |
| Differentiate | A whispered question | Ask what the outcome would do if one chosen quantity moved by the smallest amount. |
| Take an expectation | A council of possible worlds | Let each possible future speak in proportion to how often it may arrive. |

The image is never allowed to lie about the mathematics. Multiplication is not
called a lock and key merely because it sounds beautiful: the image predicts
that if either factor is zero, the whole path closes. A sum is a chorus because
separate contributions survive and join; a product would behave differently.
Whenever the image stops predicting the calculation, abandon the image and
return to the concrete case.

## How to read an excavation

Do not begin by memorizing the bold name. Cover it if necessary.

First, inhabit the scene. Make the tempting decision yourself. Before reading
on, predict what the instrument will write. When the counterexample arrives,
name exactly what disappeared. Then state the smallest repair in ordinary
language. If the equation appears and you cannot point from every term and
operation back to something you saw happen, the excavation is not finished.

At the end, enter the laboratory. Rebuild the failure before rebuilding the
accepted method. Change one input and predict the consequence before running
the code. The program is not an answer key; it is another landscape in which
your idea must survive.

The destination is not the ability to recite mathematics. It is the quieter
ability to lose a formula and invent it again.
