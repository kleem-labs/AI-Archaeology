# Mistakes Worth Preserving — Excavation 217

## The tempting idea

We tried to compare only how well each animal explains the print and choose the largest likelihood.

## The evidence that refused it

likelihood ignores how common each animal was before the evidence. A moderately diagnostic clue could make an extremely rare story look certain if prior plausibility is discarded.

## What the wreckage taught us

The next construction had to multiply each prior belief by that story's support for the evidence, then divide by the total support across all stories so the surviving weights again form one distribution.

Keep this wrong idea. It is the negative space around Conditional Probability and Bayes’ Rule: it records why the accepted method has exactly the responsibilities it does.
